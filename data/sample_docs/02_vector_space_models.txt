# Vector Space Models and Term Weighting in Information Retrieval

The Vector Space Model (VSM), pioneered by Gerard Salton, is an algebraic framework for representing textual documents as multi-dimensional vectors in a geometric space defined by the vocabulary.

In the standard Bag of Words (BoW) paradigm, word order and grammatical syntax are discarded, preserving only the occurrences of vocabulary terms. To quantify the relevance of a document to an ad-hoc query, terms are assigned weights.

Term Frequency (TF) measures how frequently a term occurs within a specific document:
TF(t, d) = count(t, d)

However, raw term frequency overvalues common terms. Inverse Document Frequency (IDF) mitigates this by penalizing terms that appear across many documents in the corpus:
IDF(t, D) = ln((1 + |D|) / (1 + df(t))) + 1

The composite TF-IDF score balances local relevance against global distinctiveness:
TF-IDF(t, d) = TF(t, d) * IDF(t, D)

To eliminate bias favoring longer documents, vectors are projected onto a unit sphere using Euclidean L2 normalization. The similarity between a query vector and a document vector is measured by the cosine of the angle between them:
Cosine Similarity = (q . d) / (||q|| * ||d||)

When vectors are normalized to unit length, cosine similarity simplifies directly to the Euclidean inner product.
