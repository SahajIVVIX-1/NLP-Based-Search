# Natural Language Processing: Principles and Foundations

Natural Language Processing (NLP) is a multidisciplinary domain at the intersection of computer science, artificial intelligence, and computational linguistics. Its core objective is to enable computational systems to understand, interpret, analyze, and generate human languages in a meaningful way.

Classical NLP architectures rely heavily on a sequential preprocessing pipeline:
1. Document Ingestion: Converting raw character streams into standardized text strings.
2. Sentence Segmentation and Word Tokenization: Deconstructing continuous prose into discrete semantic units termed tokens.
3. Text Normalization: Case-folding uppercase letters to lowercase, stripping punctuation marks, and removing non-alphanumeric noise.
4. Stopword Removal: Filtering out ubiquitous grammatical words such as 'the', 'is', 'at', and 'which', which carry minimal topical discrimination.
5. Morphological Stemming: Reducing inflected or derived words to their morphological root base using algorithmic rule engines like the Porter Stemmer. For example, terms such as 'searching', 'searched', and 'searches' collapse into the single root 'search'.

While modern developments have expanded into neural representations, understanding classical tokenization, vocabulary distributions, and lexical analysis remains foundational to the entire discipline of computational text processing.
